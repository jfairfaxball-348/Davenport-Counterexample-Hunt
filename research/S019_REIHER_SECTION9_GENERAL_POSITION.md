## CLM-0106 — Observation 9.4

For n>=5 *distinct* points in K², either at least n−1 are collinear or the space V of leading homogeneous quadratic triples (a,b,c) of equations a x²+bxy+c y²+d x+e y+f=0 through them has dim<=1. The space is the image under a linear projection of the coefficient kernel. Invertible affine changes preserve its dimension because the homogeneous quadratic part undergoes an invertible linear substitution.

Suppose neither conclusion. Let m be the maximum count on one affine line; 2<=m<=n−2, with points P1,...,Pm on that line.

**m>=3:** Affinely normalize their line to y=0. With P_i=(r_i,s_i), vanishing of each conic at three distinct (r_i,0) forces a=d=f=0: a quadratic in x has three distinct roots. Remaining equation is y(bx+cy+e)=0. Two distinct off-line points P_{m+1},P_{m+2} exist because m<=n−2; their y coordinates are nonzero, so both satisfy bx+cy+e=0. Subtracting yields b(r_{m+1}−r_{m+2})+c(s_{m+1}−s_{m+2})=0. The coefficient vector is nonzero since these two points are distinct, so the possible leading triples (0,b,c) span dimension<=1, contradiction.

**m=2:** No three are collinear, so P1,P2,P3 can be sent by an invertible affine map to O=(0,0), E1=(1,0), E2=(0,1). A quadratic through these three is a(x²−x)+bxy+c(y²−y)=0. If V has dimension>=2, its intersection with c=0 in K³ has a nonzero vector (A,B,0). Thus five distinct points lie on the zero set x(Ax+By−A)=0, the union of at most two lines. A union of two lines containing five distinct points has three on one line, contradicting m=2. This covers n=5 and n=8 without an unstated stronger hypothesis.

## CLM-0107 — Observation 9.5

Claim: seven distinct points in K³ admitting a space of quadratic-surface leading sextuples of dimension>=4 must have four collinear or six coplanar. Assume the negation. Four points must be affinely independent (otherwise all seven lie in a plane), hence normalize them to O,E1,E2,E3, the origin and three standard basis vectors. Every quadratic equation through these four takes the form
a(x²−x)+b(y²−y)+c(z²−z)+d yz+e zx+f xy=0.
Let W⊆K⁶ denote the coefficient subspace of equations through all seven. By hypothesis dim W>=4. All arguments below concern these actual seven points and all actual nice equations, not abstract quadrics chosen ad hoc.

**(*) No five are coplanar.** If five lie in one plane, choose three noncollinear among them (otherwise >=4 would lie on a line) and one outside the plane; normalize the five to x=0 with O,E2,E3 among them, and an outside point to E1. The five planar (y,z)-projections are distinct and no four are collinear. Apply just-proved 9.4 at n=5: the image of W on (b,c,d) has dimension<=1. Its kernel has dim>=3 and lies in the three-dimensional subspace of forms x(x−1),xz,xy. Hence ALL three of these forms are nice, vanishing at every one of the seven points. Their common zeros have x=0 or (x,y,z)=E1. The remaining sixth point is distinct from E1, therefore x=0; six points are coplanar, contradiction.

**(**) No three are collinear.** Suppose O,E1,uE1 occur with u not 0 or1. Choose two further original points outside their line and plane so that E2,E3 also occur; possible by (*) (otherwise at least five coplanar). Substituting uE1 into the nice equation forces a u(u−1)=0, hence a=0. Projection of W onto the triple (b,d,f) has dimension>=2 because only two other coefficients (c,e) remain. Every (b,d,f) in this projected subspace yields a nice y[b(y−1)+dz+fx]. The two residual points P4,P5 have y!=0; otherwise they would each lie in plane y=0 containing O,E1,uE1,E3, violating (*). Therefore the vectors P4−E2 and P5−E2 are annihilated by a >=2-dimensional subspace of K³ and hence are linearly dependent. They are nonzero (points distinct), so E2,P4,P5 are collinear. Symmetrically using z and E3, E3,P4,P5 are collinear. Thus E2,E3,P4,P5 form four DISTINCT collinear points, contradiction.

**(□) Triple forbidden coplanarity pattern.** The three quadruples (P1,P2,P3,P7), (P1,P2,P4,P6), (P1,P3,P4,P5) cannot all be coplanar. Otherwise normalize P1=O,P2=E1,P3=E2,P4=E3, and write P5=(0,r,s), P6=(t,0,u), P7=(v,w,0). By (**), all of r,s,t,u,v,w are nonzero (otherwise a coordinate-axis triple). Since dim W>=4, the 3-dimensional subspace of pure mixed forms d yz+e zx+f xy meets W in a nonzero member. Evaluating at P5,P6,P7 gives d rs=e tu=f vw=0. Every displayed product is nonzero, so all d=e=f=0, impossible. In particular, the original printed 'e.g. d!=0' does not silently justify choosing a nonzero component; this proof covers every nonzero mixed form.

**Terminal contradiction:** Normalize O,E1,E2,E3 once more; the remaining P5,P6,P7 define one plane h because (**) excludes three collinear. By (*) this plane contains at most one of E1,E2,E3, so arrange E1,E2 not in h. Each of the three coordinate-factor subspaces of the full six-dimensional coefficient space has dimension three, e.g. x[a(x−1)+fy+ez]. Their intersection with W is nonzero, by 4+3>6. Choose a nonzero nice x L1 where L1=a(x−1)+fy+ez; the plane L1=0 contains E1. Were all three P5,P6,P7 to have x!=0, all would lie on L1=0, forcing h=L1=0 and E1∈h. Thus some P5 has x=0. Similarly a nonzero nice y L2, with E2 on L2=0, forces one of P5,P6,P7 to have y=0; it cannot be P5 because (x5,y5)=(0,0) would put O,E3,P5 on one line, contradicting (**). Call it P6. Now O,E2,E3,P5 lie in x=0 and O,E1,E3,P6 lie in y=0. If z7=0 then O,E1,E2,P7 lie in z=0: forbidden pattern (□). Thus z7!=0. Also z5!=0 since otherwise O,E2,P5 collinear; z6!=0 since otherwise O,E1,P6 collinear. Applying the z-factor subspace gives a nonzero nice z L3 with L3(E3)=0. Since z5,z6,z7 are all nonzero, h=L3=0 and therefore E3∈h. Three final quadruples (E3,O,P5,E2), (E3,O,P6,E1), (E3,P5,P6,P7) are simultaneously coplanar. They match (□) under the index permutation (1,2,3,4,5,6,7)=(E3,O,P5,P6,P7,E1,E2), contradiction. This closes Observation9.5, including all claimed coordinate divisors and dimension inequalities.

## CLM-0108 — Lemma 9.6

Take four original *distinct* A,B,C,D satisfying the FULL earlier Lemma9.2(g): fourteen indexed sums of one/two/three distinct original terms are pairwise distinct; none of the eight points from any four complementary pairs are collinear; none of the twelve from six complementary pairs lie on a **proper** conic. Definition9.3 calls them in general position if the leading-quintuple space of quartic curves through these fourteen sums has dimension <=2.

Assume contrary dimension>=3. Set T=A+B+C+D, M=T/2 (allowed by char K!=2). The seven pairs in 9.2(g) have common total T, hence are (M+P_i,M−P_i), i=1,...,7. All fourteen points are distinct. In particular P_i !=0 and P_i!=±P_j for i≠j.

Put P_i=(x_i,y_i) and Q_i=(x_i²,x_i y_i,y_i²)∈K³. The Q_i are distinct: if Q(v)=Q(w) for nonzero plane vectors, equality x²=x'² and y²=y'² and xy=x'y' forces w=±v (choose a nonzero coordinate, then the cross term fixes the common sign). This contradicts distinctness of the fourteen ±P_i. Translating the quartic-curve system by −M preserves its homogeneous degree-four part and thus dimension>=3 of the leading-quintuple space. Select three linearly independent leading quintuples (a_j,b_j,c_j,d_j,e_j) of quartics F_j vanishing on all ±P_i. The EVEN part [F_j(x,y)+F_j(−x,−y)]/2 also vanishes, has unchanged leading quintuple, and has only quartic, quadratic and constant terms:
a_j x⁴+b_j x³y+c_j x²y²+d_j xy³+e_j y⁴+f_j x²+g_j xy+h_j y²+i_j.
With coordinates (u,v,w)=(x²,xy,y²), these become quadratic surfaces
a_j u²+b_j uv+c_j v²+d_j vw+e_j w²+f_j u+g_j v+h_j w+i_j=0
through all seven Q_i. Add the surface v²−uw=0, which all Veronese points satisfy. Their FOUR leading sextuples are independent: the first three have zero uw coefficient and inject the three independent original quintuples, while the fourth has nonzero uw coefficient. Apply proven 9.5.

**Four collinear Q's.** If Q1,...,Q4 lie on a line, two independent affine-linear forms vanish on them. Pull back by Q to two conic equations αx²+βxy+γy²+δ=0 through all eight DISTINCT ±P1,...,±P4. Their leading triples are independent: a nonzero linear combination cancelling all quadratic terms would be a nonzero constant vanishing at a point, impossible, contradicting independence of the two forms. Thus for these eight points the conic leading-triple space has dimension>=2. By 9.4 with n=8, at least seven are collinear. Of four opposite pairs, >=3 complete opposite pairs lie on that line, so it passes through 0. The eighth point is opposite to one of the seven, so lies on that same line. Consequently all eight M±P_i from four original pairs are collinear, prohibited by 9.2(g).

**Six coplanar Q's.** If Q1,...,Q6 lie on an affine plane, an affine-linear equation αu+βv+γw+δ=0 vanishes there with at least one of α,β,γ nonzero (a nonzero constant equation has no points). Its pullback αx²+βxy+γy²+δ=0 is a PROPER conic through twelve ±P_i. Translating by M retains its nonzero quadratic leading part, so twelve original sums from six original complementary pairs lie on a proper conic, forbidden by 9.2(g).

Thus both 9.5 outcomes contradict precisely the earlier exclusions. Lemma9.6 holds for arbitrary K of char !=2.
