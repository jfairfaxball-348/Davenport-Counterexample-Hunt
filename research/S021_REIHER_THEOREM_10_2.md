## CLM-0110 — Every prime has Property B

**Exact definitions.** Over F_p^2, suspicious means an indexed length 2p−2 sequence with no nonempty zero-sum index subset. Cloudy means indexed length 2p−1, total sum zero, and no nonempty PROPER zero-sum index subset. Simple means a cloudy sequence isomorphic under GL_2(F_p) and permutation to p−1 copies E=(1,0) together with p points (a_i,1) whose sum is E. Property B is the classification of suspicious sequences into Reiher's types I/II, equivalently the assertion every cloudy sequence is simple. In a cloudy sequence the full index set is a zero-sum subset and must NOT be treated as forbidden.

**Prove equivalence rather than import it.** From suspicious S, append t=−sum(S). The new total is zero. A nonempty proper zero-sum subset not using t contradicts S; a subset using t would leave a nonempty complement in S summing zero, also impossible. The resulting sequence is cloudy. Conversely deleting ANY original indexed position from cloudy P gives suspiciousness. If cloudy P is simple, deleting one of its p−1 E positions yields p−2 E and p outer points of sum E (suspicious type II); deleting one outer term yields p−1 E with p−1 remaining points (a,1) (suspicious type I). Conversely, type I S has p−1 E and p−1 points (a_i,1), and cloudification adds (1−sum a_i,1), resulting in p outsiders summing E; type II S has p−2 E and p outsiders summing E, so its cloudifying term is E. This verifies classification equivalence BOTH ways with the correct indexed multiplicities.

**Small primes.** A cloudy sequence cannot contain 0 (a forbidden singleton). For p=2, p−1=1: any one original nonzero term gives p−1 dependent terms, and independently proved Observation 2.6 (CLM-0094) makes the cloudy sequence simple. For p=3, the five nonzero original terms occupy four 1-dimensional F_3-subspaces; two positions share a direction, and Corollary 2.5 (CLM-0094) forces those two ORIGINAL terms to be equal, not merely proportional. Thus p−1=2 equal terms occur, and Observation 2.6 proves simplicity. Neither case uses a numerical search.

**Now p>=5.** All primes remaining are odd, so every characteristic !=2 hypothesis of original Section 9 is satisfied. If P involves at most three DISTINCT original point-values, the fully hand-proved Lemma 7.2 (CLM-0097, dependencies CLM-0094–0096) already proves P simple. Otherwise choose four pairwise DISTINCT values A,B,C,D, represented by four different ORIGINAL indexed terms in P. They are not auxiliary points. Lemma 9.2 (CLM-0105), proved for arbitrary fields of characteristic !=2, provides at least one of its seven EXACT alternatives:

(c) Three of them are collinear. Original Proposition 5.1 (CLM-0100), independently reconstructed including p=5, applies directly to three DISTINCT ORIGINAL cloudy points, and yields simplicity.

(d) Three of them together with the origin form an affine trapezoid. Original Proposition 6.2 (CLM-0102, with Lemma6.1 CLM-0101) covers this exact geometric hypothesis and proves simplicity. (e) One original point equals the sum of the other three. Original Proposition 8.1 (CLM-0103) applies via D=A+B+C, eta=+1 to four DISTINCT original terms, proving simplicity.

(f) The four original points form a parallelogram. The SAME Proposition 8.1 (CLM-0103) applies to a relabelling with D=A−B+C, eta=−1, again with four DISTINCT originals. The two signs were independently checked and include p=5.

The seven original pairs are precisely (A,B+C+D), (B,A+C+D), (C,A+B+D), (D,A+B+C), (A+B,C+D), (A+C,B+D), (A+D,B+C). These alternatives exhaust all configurations for the selected four terms: (a),(b) contradict cloudy/distinctness and (c)–(g) each imply simple. By the independently checked cloudification equivalence, every prime has original Property B. **QED.**
