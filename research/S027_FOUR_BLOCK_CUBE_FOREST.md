## Exact scope and prerequisites

Take an odd prime p>=7, set a=3(p-1)/2 and b=(3p-1)/2=a+1. Assume, only for deriving necessary structure, that an INDEXED length 6p-3 sequence S over F_p^3 contains no nonempty zero-sum of length <=a. The internally reconstructed original Bhowmik--Schlage-Puchta 2012 Chevalley--Warning argument (CLM-0120, original publisher offprint printed pp22-24) gives a zero-sum block Z of length 3p because no p-zero-sum can occur (p<=a). The *classical source-stated*, NOT newly reconstructed, Olson D(C_p^3)=3p-2 implies the pair-cover theorem CLM-0122: for every two distinct positions i,j of Z, some balanced b-position zero-sum B contains both or neither. Also CLM-0122 proves for any three distinct balanced b-blocks some two positions are separated by all three. All positions below are indexed, even where values repeat. Every proper nonempty zero sum in Z has size b or b+1; complementary halves are atoms under no-short hypothesis.

## CLM-0123 (new necessary condition): four balanced blocks force at least six membership classes

**Theorem.** Under these prerequisites, IF the family F(Z) of indexed b-position zero-sum subsets consists of EXACTLY four distinct members B1,B2,B3,B4, then the four-bit signatures (1_{i in B1},...,1_{i in B4}) attained by i in Z have at least SIX distinct values.

**Proof.** For j=1,2,3,4, apply CLM-0122's three-block separation to the OTHER three members of F(Z). There exist distinct indices u_j,v_j with opposite membership bits on every coordinate except possibly j. The pair-cover from CLM-0122 must be witnessed by one of the four blocks. None of the other three works; hence B_j contains both or neither, and their j-th bits agree. Thus their four-bit signatures are endpoints of an edge of the 4-cube at Hamming distance exactly three, with UNIQUE unchanged coordinate j. Choose one such edge E_j for each j.

Work over F_2 ONLY on these binary membership signatures, not on the group values. The XOR displacement vector of E_j is d_j=1+e_j in F_2^4: entries one in each of the other three coordinates and zero in coordinate j. The four vectors d_1,...,d_4 are linearly independent, because their matrix is J-I (over the integers determinant -3, hence determinant 1 modulo 2). In any cycle among the four chosen undirected edges, XOR of its edge displacement vectors is zero. Independence rules out every cycle (including a proper subset). Consequently the graph formed by E_1,...,E_4 is a FOREST. Four edges in a forest require at least five distinct occurring signature vertices. Therefore t, the number of signature classes, satisfies t>=5.

Suppose t=5. Then the four edges form a spanning tree on these five vertices. Choose a root signature r. Flip membership bits coordinate by coordinate wherever r has a 1, sending r to 0000; this is an invertible affine row operation on the equations for zero sums (complementation uses sigma(Z)=0). Let w1,w2,w3,w4 be the four other distinct root-normalised 0-1 column vectors and let M be the 4x4 integral matrix with these columns. Over F_2, each tree edge displacement is one of the four independent vectors d_j. Tree paths from r to the four other vertices form an invertible change of basis between the four edge displacement vectors and the four root-to-vertex displacements. Therefore det M is ODD.

For clarity, an exact determinant bound requires no pattern enumeration: construct the 5x5 sign matrix H with top row and left column all +1, and lower-right block J-2M (the sign-entry version of the 0-1 matrix). Subtracting the first row from each other row gives det H = (-2)^4 det M = 16 det M. Every row of H has Euclidean norm sqrt(5). Hadamard's determinant inequality gives 16|det M|<=5^(5/2)<64, so |det M|<=3 because it is integral. As det M is odd, det M belongs to {+1,-1,+3,-3}. In particular det M is NONZERO modulo every prime p>=7. The five augmented columns (1,r), (1,w1),...,(1,w4), after affine root normalisation, are therefore independent over F_p.

For each occurring signature v let C_v be the nonempty set of indices with that signature and x_v=SUM_{i in C_v} z_i in F_p^3. The five vector zero-sum equations sigma(Z)=sigma(B1)=...=sigma(B4)=0 are exactly SUM_v (1,v)^T x_v=0 (one scalar equation per group coordinate). The 5x5 augmented incidence matrix is invertible over F_p, forcing x_v=0 for ALL five occurring classes. Since they partition the 3p indices, some C_v has size at most floor(3p/5), which is <=a for p>=7. This is a nonempty forbidden short zero sum. Contradiction. Hence t cannot equal five, and t>=6 as claimed. QED.

**Adversarial scope and first essential STOP.** The selected edges cannot form cycles, but six or more signature vertices permit dependencies among 5-dimensional augmented columns; the zero-sum equations do NOT force an individual signature-class sum zero. No classification of six-or-more signatures, no proof of impossibility of exactly four b-blocks, no assertion that F(Z) has five or more b-blocks, and no strong short-extraction theorem follows. The theorem is a NEW necessary condition only, under the source-stated Olson D1 input and previous CLM-0120/0122. The current proof does not cover all overlaps or larger b-block families.
