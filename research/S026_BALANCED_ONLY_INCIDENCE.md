## CLM-0122 — rigorous new pair-cover and triple-cut obstruction

**Theorem (necessary condition, not elimination).** Under the above hypotheses, let F(Z) be the family of indexed zero-sum subblocks of Z with exactly b positions. Then (i) for every distinct indices i,j in Z, there is B in F(Z) containing both i,j or containing neither; (ii) for every three DISTINCT blocks B1,B2,B3 in F(Z), there exist distinct positions i,j in Z with exactly one in each Br (r=1,2,3); (iii) |F(Z)|>=4.

**Proof, balanced lengths and atoms.** If W is any proper nonempty zero-sum subset of Z, Z\W is also proper, nonempty and zero-sum. By global exclusion, |W|>=b and |Z\W|>=b. Their sum is 3p=2b+1, so {|W|,|Z\W|}={b,b+1}. In particular W has only those two possible sizes. Every such W is minimal zero-sum (an atom): if it had a proper nonempty zero-sum Q then |Q|<|W|; if |W|=b, then |Q|<=b−1=a, forbidden. If |W|=b+1, only |Q|=b remains possible and W\Q has size 1 and sum zero, contradicting the absence of zero singleton in S. Thus both pieces of any balanced split are atoms.

**Proof (i), two-deletion indexed pair-cover.** For any different i,j in Z, remove exactly these two indexed positions; the remaining indexed sequence has length 3p−2. SOURCE_STATED Olson D1(Cp³)=3p−2 yields a nonempty zero-sum W therein. W is a PROPER zero sum in Z, so its size is b or b+1. If |W|=b, it is in F(Z) and omits i,j. If |W|=b+1, the complement B=Z\W is in F(Z), and contains both i,j. This proves (i), including all repeated-value cases. It also shows F(Z) is nonempty.

**Proof (ii), 3-bit incidence matrix.** Fix three DISTINCT b-index zero-sums B1,B2,B3. Suppose for contradiction that no pair of indices is separated by ALL THREE. Assign to each position t in Z the bit vector v(t)=(1_{t∈B1},1_{t∈B2},1_{t∈B3})∈{0,1}³. The supposition is precisely that no two OCCURRING signatures are complementary bitwise. Consequently at most four distinct signatures occur, one from each of the four complementary pairs in the 3-cube. Since 0<|Br|=b<3p, every coordinate is nonconstant on occurring signatures.

Let t be the number of distinct occurring signatures and C_v the nonempty index class with signature v; write x_v=Σ_{i∈C_v} z_i∈F_p³. Because σ(Z)=σ(B1)=σ(B2)=σ(B3)=0, we have the vector equations Σ_v x_v=0 and Σ_v v_j x_v=0 for j=1,2,3. If t<=3, the augmented columns (1,v) for distinct 3-cube vertices are linearly independent in characteristic odd: three distinct binary vertices cannot be collinear over F_p (the varying coordinate forces any line parameter corresponding to a third binary vertex to be 0 or 1). For t=1 or 2, nonconstancy of all three coordinates would force two signatures that are bitwise complementary (or a constant coordinate), already forbidden. Hence t>=3; and the t<=3 linear system forces all occurring class sums x_v=0.

If t=4, choose an occurring signature and flip its three coordinate bits to normalise it to 000. This applies invertible affine row operations to the four zero equations (a complement coordinate has zero sum since σ(Z)=0), preserves nonconstancy and absence of antipodes. The other three signatures must choose ONE of each of the complementary pairs {100,011},{010,101},{001,110}. Let r be the number of weight-two choices. For r=0, the determinant of the three difference columns is ±1; for r=2 it is ±1; for r=3 it is ±2. For r=1 the three nonzero signatures are e_i,e_j,e_i+e_j, so the remaining coordinate is ZERO on all four signatures, contradicting nonconstancy of that coordinate. Thus in every admissible case the four augmented columns are independent over F_p because p is ODD. Therefore x_v=0 for every occurring signature also when t=4.

There are t>=3 disjoint NONEMPTY classes partitioning |Z|=3p positions. Some class has 1<=|C_v|<=floor(3p/t)<=p<=a. But its sum x_v=0, contradicting the no-short assumption. This proves (ii) with no finite-field computation, numerical case enumeration or software.

**Proof (iii), at least four distinct b blocks.** If F(Z) had only one member B, choose i∈B and j∈Z\B: this pair is separated by its only block, contradicting (i). If it had two DISTINCT members B,C, put r=|B∩C|. When r>0, select i∈B∩C and j∈Z\(B∪C); the latter exists because |Z\(B∪C)|=(2b+1)−(2b−r)=r+1>=2. When r=0, select i∈B and j∈C. In both cases the pair is separated by BOTH available blocks, contradicting (i). If F(Z) had exactly three members, (ii) supplies a pair separated by all three, contradicting (i). Hence |F(Z)|>=4. QED.

**Exactly what this does NOT prove.** The theorem only forces multiple balanced b-zero-subsets. For |F(Z)|>=4, each triple must have a fully separated pair, but that pair may be co-contained/co-excluded by a FOURTH b-zero-subset. The three-bit affine rank argument cannot eliminate these larger incidence signatures: more than four distinct patterns may occur and the four equations alone do not force each individual pattern-cell sum zero. This is the FIRST UNCLOSED ESSENTIAL PREMISE in Route A. No impossibility of balanced-only Z, no symbolic p>=7 counterexample family, no stronger s_{<=a}, no Dk or D1 advancement is asserted.
